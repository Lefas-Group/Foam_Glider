What to do if I can only print A4?

The same parts, re-nested onto A4 instead of tiled across it, and written out as a two-page 1:1 PDF. The foam is unchanged — it is still the A3 sheet the brief specifies. Only the paper changes.

TipSpecified

Re-nest onto A4 rather than tile the A3 across two sheets · decided here, 2026-08-30 · two A4 portrait sheets side by side come to exactly A3, so tiling works in principle, but every printer refuses to print to the paper edge and the seam falls across parts. Re-nesting costs nothing because the only part too big for A4 is the wing, which the build already splits at the centreline.

Answer. Print two A4 sheets — one portrait, one landscape — and cut nothing across a seam. Every part fits a single sheet except the wing, which at 300 mm misses A4’s 297 mm edge by 3 mm. That costs nothing, because the wing is divided at its centreline during assembly anyway: each panel is 150 × 98.8 mm and fits easily.

Two A4 portrait sheets butted together are exactly A3, so the A3 plan could be tiled instead. It is a worse answer. Printers leave an unprintable margin of several millimetres, so the join loses material precisely where the wing and the long fuselage pieces cross it, and those are the parts whose length matters most. Re-nesting has no seam at all.

Cut the two wing panels as a matched pair — stack the foam and cut both at once — since they no longer come from one piece split down the middle.

Download the 1:1 A4 cutting plan (2 pages) — print at 100%, and check a wing panel measures 150 mm along its straight edge before cutting. Everything else — stations, dihedral, ballast — is unchanged from the A3 plan.

NoteThe method, as called 
def sym_surface(eta, chord, semi):
    """
    A symmetric flat pattern [mm]: straight leading edge, chord table swept to
    both tips. Span along x, chord along y -- paper is landscape, so are wings.
    """
    y = np.concatenate([-eta[::-1], eta[1:]]) * semi * 1e3
    c = np.concatenate([chord[::-1], chord[1:]]) * 1e3
    return np.column_stack([np.concatenate([y, y[::-1]]),
                            np.concatenate([np.zeros_like(y), c[::-1]])])

def nest(parts, layout, sheet):
    """
    Place parts at given bottom-left corners and check the packing.

    Raises rather than returning a flag: a template that overhangs the sheet or
    overlaps itself is worse than no template, and it should stop the render.
    """
    placed = {n: p - p.min(axis=0) + np.array(layout[n]) for n, p in parts.items()}
    bb = lambda p: (p[:, 0].min(), p[:, 1].min(), p[:, 0].max(), p[:, 1].max())
    for n, p in placed.items():
        x0, y0, x1, y1 = bb(p)
        if x0 < 0 or y0 < 0 or x1 > sheet[0] or y1 > sheet[1]:
            raise ValueError(f"{n} falls off the {sheet[0]:.0f}x{sheet[1]:.0f} sheet")
    names = list(placed)
    for i, a in enumerate(names):
        for b in names[i + 1:]:
            ax0, ay0, ax1, ay1 = bb(placed[a])
            bx0, by0, bx1, by1 = bb(placed[b])
            if ax0 < bx1 and bx0 < ax1 and ay0 < by1 and by0 < ay1:
                raise ValueError(f"{a} overlaps {b}")
    return placed
