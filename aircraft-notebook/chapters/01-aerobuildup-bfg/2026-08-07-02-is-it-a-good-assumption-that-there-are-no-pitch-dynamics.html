Is it a good assumption that there are no pitch dynamics, at this level of fidelity?

Longitudinal modal timescales at the best-glide condition, then the same launch released from 2 m flown twice on one aero table — once with angle of attack pinned at trim, once with pitch attitude and pitch rate as free states.

Pitch inertia comes from the mass build-up below, which distributes the assumed 12 g all-up mass. The CG is assumed to sit at the model’s moment reference. The stabilizer incidence that would trim the aircraft is not modelled, so it is stood in for by a constant pitching-moment offset. The aero table is clamped at 16°, above which the section data would be extrapolated, so excursions beyond that angle are held at the 16° coefficients.

Answer. For a release at trim speed, yes — the two models agree to within 0.1%, and angle of attack never leaves 5.7°–6.5°. For a launch above trim speed, no. The short period is not the reason: it is fast and well damped. The reason is kinematic — angle of attack is the difference between pitch attitude and flight path angle, and over the top of a zoom the flight path drops faster than the airframe pitches down, driving angle of attack past stall. A fixed angle of attack cannot represent that excursion at any level of pitch damping.

# Short period from the two-state [alpha, q] approximation; phugoid from the
# standard Lanchester result, whose damping is set entirely by L/D.
q_dyn = 0.5 * rho * V_trim ** 2
Za = q_dyn * S_w * CLa / (m * V_trim)                       # 1/s
Ma = q_dyn * S_w * c_w * Cma / Iyy                          # 1/s^2
Mq = q_dyn * S_w * c_w ** 2 * Cmq / (2 * V_trim * Iyy)      # 1/s

w_sp = (-Ma - Za * Mq) ** 0.5
z_sp = (Za - Mq) / (2 * w_sp)
w_ph = g * 2 ** 0.5 / V_trim
z_ph = 1 / (2 ** 0.5 * LD)

# One aero table over (alpha, V), so both trajectory models see identical
# aerodynamics and the only difference between them is the pitch freedom.
al_grid = onp.linspace(-6, 16, 45)
V_grid = onp.array([2.0, 3.0, 4.0, 5.0, 6.5, 8.0, 10.0, 12.5, 15.0, 18.0])

CL_t = onp.zeros((len(al_grid), len(V_grid)))
CD_t, Cm_t = onp.zeros_like(CL_t), onp.zeros_like(CL_t)
for j, V in enumerate(V_grid):
    p = polars(al_grid, V=float(V))
    CL_t[:, j], CD_t[:, j], Cm_t[:, j] = p["CL"], p["CD"], p["Cm"]

Cm_offset = -float(polars(np.array([a_trim]), V=V_trim)["Cm"][0])

kw = dict(bounds_error=False, fill_value=None)
fCL = RegularGridInterpolator((al_grid, V_grid), CL_t, **kw)
fCD = RegularGridInterpolator((al_grid, V_grid), CD_t, **kw)
fCm = RegularGridInterpolator((al_grid, V_grid), Cm_t + Cm_offset, **kw)


def coeffs(al_deg, V):
    """Clamped at the table edges -- see the note on 16° above."""
    pt = onp.array([[onp.clip(al_deg, -6, 16), onp.clip(V, 2.0, 18.0)]])
    return float(fCL(pt)[0]), float(fCD(pt)[0]), float(fCm(pt)[0])


def fly(V0, pitching, dt=0.0005):
    """
    Level release at V0 from h0.

    pitching=False pins alpha at trim, so only V and the flight path angle are
    states. pitching=True adds pitch attitude and pitch rate, making alpha the
    difference between attitude and flight path.
    """
    x, z, V, gam = 0.0, h0, V0, 0.0
    th, qr = onp.radians(a_trim), 0.0
    xs, zs, als = [x], [z], [a_trim]
    t = 0.0
    while z > 0 and t < 60:
        al = onp.degrees(th - gam) if pitching else a_trim
        CL, CD, Cm = coeffs(al, V)
        q_ = 0.5 * rho * V ** 2
        L, D = q_ * S_w * CL, q_ * S_w * CD
        if pitching:
            M = q_ * S_w * c_w * (Cm + Cmq * qr * c_w / (2 * max(V, 0.5)))
            qr += (M / Iyy) * dt
            th += qr * dt
        V = max(V + ((-D - W * onp.sin(gam)) / m) * dt, 0.5)
        gam += ((L - W * onp.cos(gam)) / (m * V)) * dt
        x += V * onp.cos(gam) * dt
        z += V * onp.sin(gam) * dt
        xs.append(x); zs.append(z); als.append(al); t += dt
    return onp.array(xs), onp.array(zs), onp.array(als)
