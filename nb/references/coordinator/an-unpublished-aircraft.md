# An aircraft nobody published

Some aircraft have no manufacturer. The user designed this one and built it,
and then ran `nb intake` — so the photographs in `_reference/` are frames they
shot, and `_reference/INTAKE.yml` holds figures they measured, which `new`
merges into the brief. You can see which rows those were: `SOURCES.txt` heads
them `measured by the user`.

**That inverts the usual order, and the inversion is the whole page.** Setting
up a published aircraft, your first job is to find figures somebody else
printed. Here there are none to find. Do not `search` for a product page, do
not `search` for frames, and above all do not retype what intake already holds
— `new` has it, and a row typed twice is a row that can disagree with itself.

## What still has to be researched

The materials. They are published even when the aircraft is not, and they are
where this fails silently: foam areal density, and the mass of every motor,
ESC, servo, propeller and battery named. A target rests on a constant, and a
constant nobody supplied is one the run invents and attributes. `sources.md`
carries the measurement — a run guessed 0.40 kg/m² against a real 0.293 and
the entry it committed was worthless.

So the research is still yours to do. It is just research into the bill of
materials rather than into the airframe.

## Do not invent a target to fill the block

`the-brief.md` holds the rule and the F-16 that broke it. The danger is worse
here: a published figure is a second opinion, and an aircraft nobody published
has none. A target the user did not state is a number the run will solve
backwards to hit, and the entry then reports the model agreeing with itself
with nothing anywhere to contradict it.

An empty `targets:` block is an honest brief. A target you composed is not.

## The overlay is now the ONLY external check

A silhouette is what judges a mask, and `reference-photographs.md` says why
no number can. On a published aircraft that judgement is one among several,
because the targets still compare the model against figures from outside it.
Here nothing does.
**Every external check this reconstruction has is a silhouette**, which means
the angular spread stops being good practice and becomes the entire
verification: a shape fault survives in exactly the direction nobody
photographed, and no number will ever find it.

Read that page before you accept an assumptions prompt, and open every image
it lists.

## A gap in the spread is fixable HERE, and nowhere else

This is the one advantage of the situation, and it is a real one. On a
published aircraft a missing viewpoint is missing for good — the photographs
that exist are the photographs that exist. Here the aeroplane is on the user's
bench and the camera is in their pocket.

So when `reference` shows you two frames from much the same place, or you
realise nothing shows the underside, **`escalate` a specific reshoot**:

    one frame from directly below, wings level, whole aircraft in frame

Name the camera position, not the problem. Then `wait`. A reshoot costs them a
minute and buys a direction the fit is otherwise blind in; working around it
costs a run and buys a number you cannot trust.

## Anything unmeasured that sets scale is a Specified input

`judging-a-run.md` defines one: a question where a different answer changes
what is being built. A dimension the user did not measure is exactly that, and
with no product page there is no second source to settle it. Never guess one.
`escalate`, and ask for the quantity by name and in the units you want it in —
they have the aeroplane and a tape measure, so the answer is a minute away.
