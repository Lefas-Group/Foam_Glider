Pairwise mask IoU, on nb's own centroid-centred measure:

                studio  nose-on  above-behind  below-front
  studio          --      0.37       0.39          0.50
  nose-on        0.37      --        0.52          0.36
  above-behind   0.39     0.52        --           0.40
  below-front    0.50     0.36       0.40           --

These rose from a 0.20-0.47 spread when the three in-flight masks were
re-cut on saturation. The earlier, lower numbers flattered the diversity:
masks that are each wrong in a different way disagree more than masks that
are each right.

The fifth frame, 10573 `merica_painted_480`, is the SAME viewpoint as
`studio` at IoU 0.82 -- one shoot, one camera, a paint scheme added
between frames -- so it is not in the notebook. Its mask was perfect and
that bought nothing; a second overlay from the same pose fits the same
pose and hides the same faults.

Masks: all four cut by `nb mask`, which runs the pinned
`birefnet-general-lite` session (BiRefNet, MIT, via rembg). Nothing here is
thresholded or bounded by hand any more.

They were cut three times, and the history is the reason the tool exists.
The FIRST pass used brightness and greenness, lost a whole wing panel from
each in-flight frame, and was patched with local brightness rules until the
boundary traced the airframe. It was still wrong: the shaded belly, chin,
legs and wheels lay OUTSIDE all three masks, because a shaded white facet in
sunlight is a warm cream that both axes score as foliage -- on `nose-on` the
shaded belly is lum 107 against foliage at lum 100. The SECOND pass cut on
saturation, which does separate them, and fixed that. The THIRD is this one.

A bake-off over all 13 masks in the repo settled it: birefnet-general-lite
scored IoU 0.950 against them and 0.992 against `studio`, whose mask was the
vendor's own alpha and so exactly right, with a boundary cleaner than the
hand cuts -- 1.5% against 3.0% on the 5 px smoothing test. Per frame here,
model against the hand cut it replaced: studio 0.992, above-behind 0.973,
below-front 0.935, nose-on 0.771. The last is low because the HAND cut was
wrong there, not the model: it bounded the backlit port wing with a
fabricated straight bar and left ragged spurs off the starboard tip.

The inspection that caught every one of these was tinting the mask INTERIOR
over the photograph rather than tracing its edge. A boundary reads as correct
wherever it follows a real edge, and the crease between a lit facet and a
shaded one is a real edge.

Aircraft description: the FT Little Piggy is a 2026 Flite Test kit
celebrating the Flying Pig mascot, released for the DARPA Lift
Challenge at Flite Fest 2026. The fuselage is a faceted low-poly pig;
there is no horizontal tail; the ears and the wingtip endplates are the
only vertical surfaces. Flite Test describe it as "intentionally
unconventional" and "aerodynamically challenging", and shipped a custom
Aura 5 gyro tune to make it flyable.
