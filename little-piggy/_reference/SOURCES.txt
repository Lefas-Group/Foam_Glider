Pairwise mask IoU, on nb's own centroid-centred measure:

                studio  nose-on  above-behind  below-front
  studio          --      0.37       0.39          0.50
  nose-on        0.37      --        0.52          0.36
  above-behind   0.39     0.52        --           0.40
  below-front    0.50     0.36       0.40           --

These rose from a 0.20-0.47 spread when the three in-flight masks were
re-cut on saturation. The earlier, lower numbers flattered the diversity:
masks that are each wrong in a different way disagree more than masks that
are each right.

The fifth frame, 10573 `merica_painted_480`, is the SAME viewpoint as
`studio` at IoU 0.82 -- one shoot, one camera, a paint scheme added
between frames -- so it is not in the notebook. Its mask was perfect and
that bought nothing; a second overlay from the same pose fits the same
pose and hides the same faults.

Masks: `studio` uses the vendor's own alpha channel, so it is exact. The
three in-flight frames were cut by hand against the frame, and each `.txt`
records which rule cut it and which region needed a local override.

They were cut TWICE. The first pass used brightness and greenness, lost a
whole wing panel from each frame, was patched with local brightness rules
until the boundary traced the airframe, and was accepted on the strength of
that boundary drawn over the photograph. It was still wrong: the shaded
belly, chin, legs and wheels lay OUTSIDE all three masks, and a patch of sky
inside one. A shaded white facet in sunlight is a warm cream, which is what
both of those axes score as foliage -- on `nose-on` the shaded belly is
lum 107 against foliage at lum 100.

SATURATION is the axis that separates them, because white foam is achromatic
by construction however dark it gets while foliage is chromatic however
bright: 0.34 against 0.51 on `nose-on`, 0.38 against 0.79 on `above-behind`.
Re-cut on saturation, the share of each boundary that is sub-5px noise fell
from 14.4%, 2.6% and 1.7% to 3.8%, 0.9% and 0.4%, against 0.6% for the
vendor's own alpha.

The inspection that caught it was tinting the mask INTERIOR over the
photograph rather than tracing its edge. A boundary reads as correct
wherever it follows a real edge, and the crease between a lit facet and a
shaded one is a real edge.

Aircraft description: the FT Little Piggy is a 2026 Flite Test kit
celebrating the Flying Pig mascot, released for the DARPA Lift
Challenge at Flite Fest 2026. The fuselage is a faceted low-poly pig;
there is no horizontal tail; the ears and the wingtip endplates are the
only vertical surfaces. Flite Test describe it as "intentionally
unconventional" and "aerodynamically challenging", and shipped a custom
Aura 5 gyro tune to make it flyable.
